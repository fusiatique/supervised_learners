Minimal Dependence Model

A Q-learning agent keeps an internal level H near its setpoint. Each drug use lowers the baseline B that H falls back to. Once B is below the tipping point, natural rewards can no longer reach the setpoint and the drug becomes the agent’s best option.

Life events

Let the agent learn natural rewards first (a few thousand steps at high speed), then make the drug available.

A forced dose raises H and lowers B, but the agent did not choose it and learns nothing from it directly.

Understanding the states

Internal level H
The agent’s internal level right now. It learns actions that bring this closer to its target.
Baseline B
The level H drifts toward. Drug use lowers it; healing gradually restores it.
Setpoint S
The target internal level the agent tries to maintain: 50.
Tipping point
When baseline B falls below this threshold, natural rewards alone can no longer maintain the target. It is not an automatic addiction switch.
Natural-reward ceiling
The level H could maintain by choosing natural reward every step at the current baseline.

Blue shading shows the range of H within each plotted interval. Red marks show chosen drug use; dark marks show forced doses.

## Script and visual alignment

### Video 01 — human decisions and computation

The human/computer contrast and the final action–outcome–learning link fit the narration's progression. No equally clear rendering defect was found in the sampled frames. The IF/ELSE example is relatively brief, around 12–14 seconds. If viewers are expected to read it, give those two lines more visual dwell time by redistributing existing time rather than automatically lengthening the video. Keep the chosen closing question; it already leads into Video 2.

### Video 02 — consequences and safer choices

The movement from an uncertain outcome to a safer choice, then continued use despite warnings, follows the supplied script well. The unknown box could make its attraction more obvious: a faint possible reward or a small uncertainty cue would explain why the person chooses it before the negative consequence appears. This is an optional clarification, not a demonstrated transition bug.

### Video 03 — addiction and reward learning

The accumulating harms and repeated returns to the pill support “continued use despite harm.” The mouse's one-expected-versus-three-received example is particularly useful for the supplied definition of reward learning. Preserve that example. The main improvement is its exit into the older two-row comparison, described above, rather than introducing yet another example.

### Video 03.5 — dopamine, reinforcement, motivation, and prediction error

The messenger, everyday experiences, cookie reinforcement, and “not quite the pleasure molecule” correction follow the narration. The avoid-harm illustration only begins its movement shortly before the cut at 52 seconds: it is visible around 51.2–51.8 seconds, then disappears. Reward seeking receives much more time to resolve. Start the avoidance movement sooner within the existing window, or let its action finish before transitioning. The blank cuts and inconsistent cocaine container are the main visual fixes.

The storm/vignette around the cocaine example is a noticeably different visual style from the other scenes. Because it accompanies the “darker side” line, it is a defensible creative choice. Simplifying the large clipped background shapes would improve consistency, but this is optional.

### Video 04 — system versus individual pathways

The provided section 4 explicitly discusses drug diversity and enhanced mesocorticolimbic dopamine activity. It does not explicitly introduce the mesolimbic branch or list food, drink, and sex, even though those are animated. Earlier discussion mentioned that these examples belong in the narration, so confirm that the uploaded script has not omitted those lines before removing anything. A short phrase introducing the VTA → NAc branch would help viewers distinguish the broad system from the named pathway.

During the mesolimbic emphasis, the PFC remains visually prominent. Dimming its non-focused outline/fill a little more would make the VTA → NAc selection clearer. Preserve the recently added region and pathway pulses.

### Video 05 — VTA, NAc, firing, prediction error, experiments, and inputs

The food-delivery example is a good concrete match for the narration's doorbell/reward explanation. The problematic stacked comparison charts previously shown around 1:06 are absent from the current export. Preserve that cleanup.

Fix the overlapping VTA/NAc titles and the remaining long NAc leader. The final input overview, around 1:54–2:11, asks viewers to track several names and connections at once. The sequential highlights help; make each current label brighter while leaving previous inputs visible but subdued. Small explanatory captions in the spike-train and rodent views may need a readability pass at the actual presentation size.

### Video 06 — mesocortical pathway and planning

The puzzle bridge and the progression from value to choosing an action fit the script. The rodent comparison is also well aligned: the visual change occurs in incoming signals while the outgoing projection remains stable, which matches the narration's wording about signals received by projection-defined neurons. Keep that distinction. Correct the cropped PFC during planning rather than redesigning this section.

### Video 07 — control, D2 association, and prelimbic cortex

The control/brake metaphor, receptor comparison, and later rat experiment follow the supplied section. However, approximately 37–46 seconds shows stronger drug-cue reactivity and weaker natural-reward response. That material is not explicitly narrated in the uploaded Video 7 script; it corresponds more directly to the supplied Video 8 paragraph about lasting synaptic changes. Either keep Video 7 on control/urges and move smoothly toward the prelimbic explanation, or add a deliberate narration bridge if this extra material is intended to stay. Avoid automatically duplicating it in both videos.

The new PFC pulse is useful. Its strength should continue to respect the existing weaker-activity depiction, rather than making “lower PFC activity” look visually stronger.

### Video 08 — synaptic plasticity and persistence

The before/after LTP and LTD illustrations, particularly the equal test inputs, support the script well. Keep them. The main definite defect is the overlapping pair of titles around 35 seconds. Standardize the cocaine icon as noted above.

The NAc/PFC inset connectors are more circuitous than the simple labels: the NAc connection routes below the brain to the right-hand panel. This avoids crossing the anatomy but still requires eye travel. An optional cleaner layout would put the detailed regional views below their corresponding anchors, with short leaders, if that can fit without crowding the network and captions.

## Cross-video improvements

- Preserve the good handoffs between the repeated motifs: human choices, cue/reward learning, neuronal signals, and anatomical pathways.
- Standardize cocaine, cue, natural-reward, and information icons. Generic drug symbolism can remain different where the script is deliberately nonspecific.
- Make the meaning of color consistent or locally explicit. In some whole-brain views blue emphasizes the mesocortical route, while in the rodent schematic blue depicts incoming signals and both outgoing projections are gold. These uses are not inherently wrong, but a small local legend or consistent category styling would reduce ambiguity.
- Keep one readable primary title at a time. The clear examples of overlapping titles in Videos 5 and 8 suggest that heading fades should be checked separately from scene fades throughout.
- Prefer a shared visual anchor through transitions over a brief empty screen followed by redrawing everything.
- Check small labels and citations on the actual projector/display. Frame-sheet thumbnails were used for overview; small text was not classified as unreadable solely because it looked small in those thumbnails.
